Managing Dual-Strategy Venture Platforms: Equity and Credit Alignment

Leib Bolel • Managing Partner, Lioncrest
Oct 12, 2026 • 6 min read • 6 mins left
Executive Summary (TL;DR)
  • Emerging technology markets require flexible capital solutions—traditional venture equity alone can cause unnecessary founder dilution.
  • Integrating structured credit (Prospeq) and growth equity under a unified platform provides founders with optimized capital efficiency.
  • For Limited Partners, a dual-strategy platform offers risk-mitigated yields alongside breakout venture upside.

Technology platforms and enterprise software startups face increasingly complex capital decisions. While early-stage venture equity remains the standard tool to fund high-growth strategies, mature cash-flow positive companies require structured finance that doesn't dilute founder ownership. At Lioncrest, we built a dual-strategy platform combining equity and credit to fund companies dynamically at either speed.

1. The Evolution of the VC Capital Stack

Historically, venture capital firms acted solely as equity partners, writing checks for seed and Series A rounds in exchange for substantial stock. In highly regulated verticals—like fintech, cybersecurity, and digital health—platforms require early equity runway. However, once product-market fit is established and recurring revenues begin to scale, relying purely on venture equity to fund working capital or physical logistics runs becomes highly dilutive for the founding team.

By introducing structured credit options under the same management platform, we allow companies to balance high-dilution equity with lower-cost recurring-revenue debt financing, matching the funding source to the specific capital requirement.

2. Structuring Credit for Growth Technology

Underwriting debt for software and technology platforms differs significantly from traditional commercial bank credit. Traditional bank facilities rely on hard assets or physical collateral. In contrast, our credit underwriting models focus on analyzing contract visibility, customer retention cohorts, unit margins, and market vertical metrics.

This cash-flow-driven methodology allows platforms (such as AptDeco or Compliance.ai) to utilize non-dilutive credit facilities to scale physical logistics, acquire competitors, or extend runway during volatile public market equity cycles.

3. LP Sourcing Flywheel and Risk Management

For Limited Partners (LPs), investing in an emerging manager that runs a dual-strategy platform addresses a key portfolio allocation need: achieving predictable yield-generating returns from credit assets while preserving high-conviction equity upside. Furthermore, this dual model establishes a proprietary sourcing flywheel: companies backed by the credit fund serve as highly qualified, transparent pipelines for future equity rounds, and vice versa. This reduces underwriting risk across the entire platform.
