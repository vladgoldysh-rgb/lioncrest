How to Scale Agency BD Beyond the Founder

Peter Kang • Managing Director, Barrel Holdings
Oct 12, 2026 • 6 min read • 6 mins left
Executive Summary (TL;DR)
  • Most professional service firms fail to scale because sales efforts are bottlenecked by the founder's time and network.
  • Scaling BD requires documenting the founder's implicit pitch, segmenting data lists, and delegating initial outreach.
  • Transitioning from relationships to process is the single most critical shift to prepare a firm for platform investment.
Skip summary and read full article

Professional service firms and creative agencies are historically difficult to scale. Unlike SaaS or productized startups, agency scaling models depend directly on specialized headcount. However, the most severe bottleneck is rarely operational capacity—it is client acquisition. In almost every agency doing less than $10M in annual revenue, the founder is the primary sales machine.

1. The Founder-Led Sales Bottleneck

When an agency starts, the founder relies on their immediate professional network, inbound referrals, and direct introductions. This works exceptionally well for the first $1M to $3M of revenue. The founder is passionate, understands the product deeply, and has the authority to make deals and adjust pricing on the fly.

However, referrals dry up. The founder's calendar becomes overloaded with managing existing accounts and supervising employees, leaving zero time for consistent outbound outreach. Sales cycles become erratic, creating a boom-and-bust cycle of client work.

2. Designing a Repeatable Outreach System

To scale BD, we must treat sales as a predictable process rather than an artistic performance. This starts with database segmentation. Instead of searching broad lists, we focus on identifying highly specific Ideal Client Profiles (ICPs) based on historical client success stories.

Once list building is systematized using precise tools, the outreach scripts are crafted to address sector-specific problems directly. We focus on showing proof of results over generic feature pitches. Initial contact is handled by a dedicated Business Development Representative (BDR), allowing the founder to focus only on final qualifying calls.

3. Preparing for Institutional Investment

From our perspective as emerging platform managers, an agency that depends entirely on its founder for sales represents a high-risk profile. If the founder steps away, revenue halts. Conversely, an agency with a documented, metrics-driven sales process—where client acquisition is predictable and independent of any single executive—is a highly attractive asset for expansion funding.
