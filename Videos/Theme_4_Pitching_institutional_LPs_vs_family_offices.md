# Theme 4: Pitching Institutional LPs vs Family Offices

## Overview
This theme explores the fundamental differences between pitching institutional LPs (endowments, foundations, pension funds) and family offices. The core insight is that these two investor types have radically different decision-making processes, time horizons, motivations, and requirements — and GPs must tailor their approach accordingly.

---

## Key Distinctions

### Institutional LPs (Endowments, Foundations, Pensions)
- **Decision-making:** Committee-based, slow, requires formal DDQ/IC approval process (can take 6–18 months)
- **Motivation:** Achieving target allocation to alternative assets, diversification, long-term endowment returns (e.g., Yale Model)
- **Check size:** Typically $2M–$50M, but rarely below $2–3M due to operational overhead
- **Requirements:** Audited financials, LP Agreement review, legal team sign-off, ESG questionnaires, ILPA-compliant reporting
- **Relationship:** Managed at the institutional level — your champion must survive internal politics and CIO transitions
- **Diligence focus:** Track record, team stability, fund governance, fee structures, co-investment rights

### Family Offices
- **Decision-making:** Often a single decision-maker (the patriarch/matriarch or hired CIO) — can move in days or weeks
- **Motivation:** Wealth preservation, co-investment access, relationship with the GP, sector alignment with family business
- **Check size:** Varies wildly — from $250K (emerging family office) to $20M+ (sophisticated single-family office)
- **Requirements:** Often lighter — they may invest based on a lunch and a deck
- **Relationship:** Highly personal — they're betting on YOU, not a committee's risk model
- **Diligence focus:** Personal alignment, co-investment opportunities, access to dealflow, the GP's personal story

## Video Analysis

### Video 1: ivYKjtCQpZs — Institutional LP Pitch Process
**Key Insights:**
- Institutional LPs move slowly by design. Building a relationship 18+ months before your fundraise is essential
- They want to see you across multiple market cycles — a single fund's track record is rarely enough
- Every institutional LP has an "investment policy statement" — understand their constraints before pitching
- Fee sensitivity is high; institutional LPs regularly negotiate on management fees and carried interest for large commitments

### Video 2: -hXNhdrv2-k — Family Office Engagement Strategy
**Key Insights:**
- Family offices want to feel special — avoid making them feel like another line item in your LP spreadsheet
- Co-investment is a major attractor. Many family offices want direct access to deals, not just fund returns
- The personal story matters enormously. Why did you start this fund? What drives you? These questions matter more to family offices than to institutions
- Family offices can be "quiet LP" allies who invest early and don't demand the same reporting burden as institutions

### Video 3: PuAP9J859jw — Hybrid Strategy for First-Time Managers
**Key Insights:**
- For Fund I, family offices are your fastest path to capital. Institutions are your goal for Fund II+
- Build your institutional LP pipeline early — attend LP conferences, publish content, get introduced through existing LP networks
- Be honest about your stage with institutions: "We're building our institutional LP base over Fund II and III" is a credible, respected position
- The worst thing a first-time manager can do is waste 6 months in a slow institutional process and fail to close

### Video 4: CX60nIMHQzk — LP Type Selection and Fund Strategy
**Key Insights:**
- Your investor base composition affects your fund's flexibility and governance
- A fund full of family offices is easier to manage but may lack the prestige signal for future institutional capital
- Institutional anchor signals quality to other LPs — worth the effort even if it's slow
- Consider LP concentration limits: institutions often require < 20% concentration to avoid triggering SEC reporting requirements

---

## Tactical Recommendations

| Aspect | Institutional | Family Office |
|--------|--------------|---------------|
| Timeline | Start 18+ months early | 1–6 months |
| Best opener | Formal introduction from another GP | Warm intro from a mutual relationship |
| Key pitch focus | Track record, governance, team | Story, vision, co-investment access |
| Follow-up cadence | Quarterly updates, formal LP letters | Regular personal check-ins |
| Red flag | Missing data room, no audits | Feeling like "just another LP" |
