# Theme 18: Running a Lean VC Fund Team

## Analysis Summary

The concept of running a "lean VC fund team" is strongly represented through the rising prominence of the Solo GP (General Partner) model, as discussed by investors like Nico (Adjacent) and Gloria (Puzzle Ventures). Both highlight how operating leanly—often as a solo entity—serves not as a limitation, but as a strategic competitive advantage in an increasingly commoditized venture capital market.

## Key Insights

### 1. Strategic Differentiation Over Scale
Rather than retrofitting a strategy into a massive fund size, lean funds focus on a precise thesis (e.g., adjacent consumer subscriptions, B2B infrastructure). Operating solo forces GPs to rely on deep conviction and unique insights. As Gloria points out, capital is fundamentally a commodity; lean operations require GPs to deliver the "best value per euro invested" by acting as highly targeted partners rather than a bloated financial institution. 

### 2. Autonomy and Speed of Execution
Without the bureaucracy of multi-stage partnerships or institutional consensus-building, lean teams move faster. Nico emphasizes that consensus-driven models at the early stage can dilute conviction. A solo structure allows for uncompromising thesis-driven decisions and the flexibility to break one's own rules (e.g., investing at a higher valuation when a breakout company emerges) without needing to convince a broader partnership.

### 3. Avoiding the "AUM Trap"
Multi-stage funds require monumental exits (e.g., $10 billion valuations) just to return their large funds, pushing them to enforce high ownership stakes and force unnatural growth. Lean funds, capped intentionally at smaller sizes (e.g., 20-25 companies per fund), don't need to chase only the rarest decacorns. This structure allows them to build collaborative rounds (co-leading A-rounds alongside other solo GPs or specialized funds) and still return significant multiples on more realistic $1-$3 billion exits.

### 4. Intentional Capacity Management (The "Bug as a Feature")
Gloria refers to being a solo GP as a "bug, not a feature" that she turns into an advantage. A lean structure explicitly caps capacity, which acts as a forcing function for quality. Instead of trying to see every deal and relying on sheer volume, lean GPs prioritize deep focus, "sitting on their hands" when necessary, and preserving mind space. They intentionally reject the pressure to be constantly "busy," enabling clearer long-term thinking and higher-quality decision-making.

### 5. Symbiotic Relationship with Founders
Founders resonate with lean GP structures because they mirror the entrepreneurial journey. Lean GPs are founders themselves who took the risk to build their own firm, distinct from salaried partners at legacy institutions. This builds immediate empathy and trust, allowing the lean GP to win deals against massive brand-name funds by offering genuine, highly engaged, and low-ego partnerships.
