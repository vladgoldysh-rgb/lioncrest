# Deal Sourcing for Solo GPs

## Introduction
The landscape of deal sourcing for solo General Partners (GPs) and early-stage angel investors is characterized by a high degree of autonomy, relationship-driven networking, and increasingly, the use of AI. Unlike traditional venture capital firms that rely on committee consensus and large analyst teams, solo GPs carve out their competitive edge through counter-consensus investments and highly leveraged social networks.

## The Solo GP Sourcing Philosophy
- **Quality and Conviction over Volume:** Solo GPs, such as Nico from Adjacent, intentionally avoid playing the high-volume deal flow game. Instead of casting the widest net, they focus their mind space on identifying "adjacent possible" macro trends. This allows them to execute thesis-driven investments with conviction, minimizing the administrative noise of assessing thousands of startups.
- **Operating at the Edges:** Zach Coelius highlights that the most lucrative opportunities for solo GPs often lie at the "edges"—ideas that appear weird but are actually viable. Being a solo decision-maker empowers the GP to bypass the traditional VC committee's tendency to drive decisions toward mediocrity. Solo GPs can act swiftly on idiosyncratic insights without needing consensus.

## Network-Driven and Proactive Sourcing
- **Leveraged Favors:** A key strategy to guarantee access to highly coveted deals is to perform "leveraged favors" for founders. Offering high-value, low-cost assistance (like key introductions) builds immense goodwill. When these founders go on to raise capital, they naturally carve out allocation for the GP who helped them early on.
- **Democratized Access via Social Media:** Platforms like Twitter play a profound role in modern deal sourcing. Investors like Ellen J. DaSilva use Twitter to observe a founder's digital footprint, track when top-tier operators leave big tech firms, and execute bold cold outreach. This proactive strategy levels the playing field, allowing solo GPs to secure allocations in highly competitive, oversubscribed rounds (e.g., Clubhouse, Fast).
- **The Core Network:** While proactive sourcing is critical, a substantial portion of high-performing deals naturally stems from long-term relationships and trusted networks—whether through alumni groups, former co-workers, or founders the GP has known for decades. 

## The Integration of AI in Deal Sourcing
- **Automating the Top of the Funnel:** AI agents (such as Tillia Impact Ventures' "Pip") are actively being integrated into VC operations. They scrape databases, process messy pitch decks into structured briefs, and surface relevant deals that match the GP's investment criteria, dramatically accelerating the initial triage phase.
- **The Danger of Over-Automation:** A recurring concern among investors is that while AI effectively handles administrative sorting, it should not be trusted to make disqualification decisions autonomously. AI models are often "confidently wrong" and can easily filter out startups that look weak on paper but possess the unquantifiable grit and "weirdness" that signify a massive opportunity.
- **Preserving the Human Moat:** As AI commoditizes intelligence and administrative speed, a solo GP's ultimate advantage remains entirely human. Empathy, reputational trust, and the ability to personally connect with and assess the durability of a founder will remain the defining factors that enable a GP to win a deal.

## Conclusion
For solo GPs, exceptional deal sourcing is not about out-competing mega-funds on scale; it is about building deep, supportive relationships, relying on independent and often contrarian theses, and leveraging platforms like Twitter. While AI offers unprecedented acceleration in screening and intelligence gathering, the solo GP's true power lies in their uniquely human judgment and highly cultivated network.
