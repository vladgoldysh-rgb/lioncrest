# Theme 5: What LPs Look for in a Fund's Brand & Website

## Overview
In an increasingly competitive emerging manager landscape, a VC fund's brand and digital presence have become a meaningful signal for LPs during due diligence. This theme explores what LPs actually look for when they visit a fund's website, read its materials, and evaluate its public brand — and how GPs can use branding as a differentiation and trust-building tool.

---

## Video Analysis

### Video 1: X5Z2bNCOUwg — Brand as an LP Diligence Tool
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X5Z2bNCOUwg

**Key Insights:**
- LPs use a fund's website as a first pass before taking a meeting. A poor website can disqualify you before you've had a chance to pitch
- The website must clearly communicate: Who are you? What do you invest in? Why are you differentiated? These three questions should be answerable in under 60 seconds
- Team page quality matters: LPs want to see complete professional profiles, LinkedIn links, and ideally prior exits or notable investments
- Portfolio showcase is critical — if you have even 2–3 notable companies, feature them prominently
- Inconsistency between the website and deck raises red flags ("Your deck says X but your website says Y")

**What LPs want to see on a website:**
1. Clear investment thesis (sector, stage, geography)
2. Team bios with verifiable pedigree
3. Portfolio companies (with logos, not just names)
4. Contact information that actually works
5. Proof of thought leadership (writing, speaking, media)

### Video 2: VA5dYw7R4n4 — The Brand vs. Performance Trade-off
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VA5dYw7R4n4

**Key Insights:**
- LPs are clear: brand does not substitute for returns. But it does substitute for track record when track record is missing
- For first-time fund managers, a strong brand creates "presumption of credibility" — LPs enter the meeting already partially convinced
- Brand is a distribution tool for dealflow. Founders who've heard of you are more likely to take your call. LPs notice this
- The risk of over-branding: LPs sometimes see a heavily branded first-time fund manager and question whether they're a "media person" or a serious investor
- The ideal: high-quality content that demonstrates sector expertise, not personal celebrity

### Video 3: 5CafrSDZYjM — Digital Presence as Due Diligence Signal
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5CafrSDZYjM

**Key Insights:**
- LPs frequently Google GPs before meetings — they look at LinkedIn, Twitter/X, any published writing, podcast appearances, and website
- A GP with no digital presence raises questions: "Are they even active in the ecosystem?"
- Thought leadership content (newsletters, blog posts, podcasts) signals that the GP has real opinions and expertise — not just recycled VC platitudes
- Website design quality matters: a poorly designed site signals that the GP doesn't care about first impressions, which translates to LP concerns about how they'll manage investor relations
- Video content is increasingly valued — LPs want to hear and see you before they meet you

---

## What LPs Actually Check (Due Diligence Checklist)

| Element | What LPs Look For | Red Flags |
|---------|-------------------|-----------|
| Website homepage | Clear thesis, team, portfolio in < 60 sec | Vague, generic language |
| Team bios | Specific accomplishments, links | Thin bios, no verifiable data |
| Portfolio page | Named companies, notable exits | Empty or "stealth" portfolio |
| Writing/content | Specific, thoughtful sector views | Reposted content, no original ideas |
| Social media | Active, professional, consistent | Inactive, or inconsistent with pitch |
| Contact | Responsive, professional email | Generic Gmail, no response |

## Key Takeaway
Brand is the "pre-meeting" that LPs have with you before they take the actual meeting. In a world where LPs see hundreds of decks, a strong and coherent brand is what makes yours feel worth opening. But brand credibility must be backed by investment substance — LPs will always dig deeper than the surface.
