# Fundraising as an Emerging Manager: How to Source & Connect with LPs
*Author: Mia Nguyen (Medium, Nov 9, 2022)*

Fundraising for a venture firm, especially as a first-time or emerging manager, requires thinking like a salesperson and account manager. 

## Key Takeaways
1. **Target Audience:** High net-worth individuals (HNWIs) and family offices are more likely to take a bet on emerging managers. Institutional LPs (foundations, endowments) usually prefer to build relationships over a fund cycle or two before writing a larger check.
2. **Product-Market Fit:** Your fund is your product. Spend time crafting your investment thesis and brainstorm who might resonate with your approach. Develop your ideal LP profile.
3. **Network Leverage:** Brainstorm people you may know who are looking to diversify their personal portfolio, or who work at asset management firms. 
4. **Pre-qualify LPs:** Use tools like PitchBook or Preqin to identify LPs actively deploying capital and to pre-qualify whether they may be a fit prior to reaching out.
5. **Don’t ask for capital straight out:** Approach initial conversations with the intent to ask for their help and advice as opposed to their money. 

## Which types of LPs should you reach out to?
*“Think about your fund like it’s a product. What pain does your product solve? How are you uniquely positioned to solve that pain? What LPs might be interested in your product?”* — Gil Zimmermann (FXP)

You need to know your pitch, understand what investor profiles will resonate with it, and target them intentionally. Ask LPs:
- Do you invest in first-time fund managers / emerging managers?
- What is your investment philosophy?
- What are your investment criteria when evaluating new managers?

## Who invests in emerging managers?
Emerging managers in VC are loosely defined as fund managers whose assets under management (AUM) range from $25M — $100M and/or have typically raised less than three funds.

**High Net-Worth Individuals (HNWI)** and **Family Offices** are the primary LPs investing in early-stage emerging managers. HNWI investors tend to invest given a personal relationship to the fund manager. 

## How can you leverage your personal network for fundraising?
- **Accredited investors** looking to diversify their portfolios. 
- **Fund managers** who have recently closed a fund themselves. Ask them for advice and referrals. "Emerging managers know how hard it is to fundraise and people want to help you."
- **Individuals at institutional investors:** Search LinkedIn for titles like Chief Investment Officer (CIO), Director of Investments, Portfolio Manager.

## How can you build your network of LPs?
Invest in tools and reach out to LPs who are actively deploying capital:
- Pull a PitchBook report for VC funds closed in the last 12 months.
- Search PitchBook profiles for disclosed "Limited Partners".
- Research disclosed LPs on Preqin to understand their strategy and check size.

*“The best way to build an LP network is through sharing deal flow. Relationships are built on trust, and trust takes time to build. In the meantime, show them what deals you can get access to and understand what they’re interested in.”* — Triin Linamagi (Sie Ventures)

## How do you ask for money?
Don't ask for money directly. Brainstorm a list of people you know, share the news of your new endeavor, and begin asking for **help**.
- "Look at this cool new thing I’m doing. Do you know anyone I should talk to about this?"
- Treat initial conversations like a startup doing customer discovery. Learn what drives them and what they are interested in besides returns.

## How do you get connected with LPs?
1. **Direct note:** If you know someone, send a friendly note asking to reconnect. The ask is for their time, not their capital.
2. **Warm introduction:** Ask one person you know to introduce you to two other people they know.
3. **Cold outreach:** A well-structured, personalized email is powerful. Highlight their specific investments, affiliations, or recent news to show strong alignment.
